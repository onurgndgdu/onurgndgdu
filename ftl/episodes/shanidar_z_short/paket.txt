Iraq, 2018. In a cave in the mountains, archaeologists found a skull.
It had been crushed flat. Two centimetres thick.
They lifted it out in more than two hundred pieces,
and spent years putting it back together.
She was a Neanderthal woman, in her forties.
Her teeth were worn almost to the roots.
Someone had laid her on her side, one hand under her head.
Seventy-five thousand years later, they gave her a face.
They call her Shanidar Z.

=== AÇIKLAMA ===
In 2018, a team led by the University of Cambridge was excavating Shanidar Cave in Iraqi Kurdistan, the same cave where Ralph Solecki found the remains of ten Neanderthals between 1951 and 1960. They found a new one: a skull flattened to about two centimetres thick, probably by falling rock.

The skull was lifted out in more than 200 fragments and painstakingly reassembled in Cambridge. She was a Neanderthal woman in her mid-forties, around 75,000 years old, buried on her side with a hand tucked under her head. In 2024, a 3D-printed copy of her rebuilt skull became the base for a lifelike reconstruction of her face, shown in the documentary "Secrets of the Neanderthals."
