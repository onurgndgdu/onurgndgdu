## AÇIKLAMA
In 1963, a survey team from Istanbul and Chicago recorded broken carved slabs on a hill near Şanlıurfa in southeastern Turkey, and attributed them to a Byzantine cemetery. The site was largely forgotten for three decades.

In 1994, German archaeologist Klaus Schmidt looked again and recognised the slabs as the tops of T-shaped stone pillars. Excavations since 1995 have revealed monumental circular enclosures built around 9600 BCE, carved with foxes, boars, snakes and vultures, thousands of years before writing, the wheel or Stonehenge.
