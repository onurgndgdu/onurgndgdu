By the mid-1890s there was a cycling boom on both sides of the Atlantic. And a large share of the new riders were women.  @@CUT

It is difficult, now, to see what that meant.

A respectable woman in 1890 did not travel alone. She was accompanied, or she was driven, or she did not go. Her movements were visible, and they were supervised.  @@BEAT

The bicycle removed all of that in a single object. It was cheap enough to own privately. It needed no horse, no driver, no permission, and no companion. A woman could get on it and simply leave — and nobody would know where she had gone until she came back.  @@CUT

So the bicycle did not just move women around. It changed what they wore, where they went, and who knew about it.

Men rode bicycles too. In enormous numbers — more than women did. They gripped the same handlebars, climbed the same hills, and arrived with the same red strained faces.  @@CUT

A father forbidding his daughter the bicycle because he disapproved of her independence is a tyrant. A father forbidding it because a physician has warned of permanent disfigurement is being careful.

It did not work.  @@BEAT

Women kept riding, in rising numbers, straight through the years the warnings were loudest.  @@CUT

She was not talking about exercise. She was talking about the thing everyone involved understood perfectly well, on both sides of the argument.

The bicycle was not a health question. It never had been.
